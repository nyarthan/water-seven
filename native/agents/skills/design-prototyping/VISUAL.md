# Visual prototypes

Use a visual prototype when the uncertainty concerns layout, hierarchy, interaction, or presentation.

- Build meaningfully different alternatives, not cosmetic variations of one layout.
- Keep real surrounding navigation, density, data, and design-system constraints when they affect judgment.
- Reuse existing read paths where practical; stub mutations unless mutation behavior is the question.
- Make alternatives easy to compare through separate routes, a local switcher, screenshots, or another project-appropriate mechanism.
- Keep prototype controls visibly separate from the proposed product design.

Two or three strong alternatives usually teach more than many minor variants. The output is feedback about structure and trade-offs, not production-ready UI.

Completion means the reviewer can compare the alternatives in realistic context and identify the direction, combination, or unresolved question to carry forward.
